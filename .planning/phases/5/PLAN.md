# Phase 5: Meter playhead on the loop's dt - Plan 1: Playhead catches up to the target on dt

## Goal

The meters, the spectrum and the spectrogram move smoothly in step with the music while frames reach the metering reader in bursts, with the stamps still the only authority for where the music is (owner approval, 2026-11-07).

## Must be true when done

- While frames arrive in bursts, the playhead never stands still for a paint while it is behind the target, the newest queued frame's end less the delay. Two paint rates fed the same arrivals stand at the same playhead by the first paint at which both stand at the target, and before that they differ by no more than the bound in Notes.
- Whatever puts the playhead behind the target (a burst gap longer than the queued slack, a lowered delay, the paint clock running slow against the stamps), it closes that lag smoothly, faster than wall time, until it stands at the target. A delay grown mid-feed slows it smoothly until the target catches up with it, then it follows the target again.
- The playhead never moves past the target from at or behind it, never jumps, and never moves backward except at a break.
- At a break the paint restarts at the break frame, the playhead stands at the target, and every view of playback starts again, as today. Each paint still starts where the last one ended: no gap and no overlap.
- `pacePaint` reads no clock; the meter loop's `Clock` is the only source of `dt`.
- `docs/spec/timing.md` states the rule the code follows, and `scripts/gate.sh make check` prints `exit=0`.
- On the hand-back URL with music playing, the owner sees the meters, the spectrum and the spectrogram move smoothly and in time with the music, without the freeze-and-jump.

## Notes

- Method justification (D-01). The speed law, one plus the lag over the time constant, floored at zero, is the one shape that reads the ruling word for word. At the target it moves at wall time, so it follows the target. Behind it, it moves faster than wall time and reaches the target in finite time, so lag is not kept. Ahead of it, it moves slower and the speed falls continuously, so a grown delay slows it smoothly (D-03). The speed is continuous in the lag on both sides of the target. It depends on the stamps, not on a running total of `dt`, so the paint clock running slow against the stamps opens a lag the same law closes; nothing drifts. A law that slows into the target from behind would move slower than wall time while behind it, against D-01's "faster than wall time". A pure proportional law never reaches the target, so AC1 and AC3 could not hold.
- Time constant, 500 ms. With measured bursts of 5 events, about 107 ms of playback, a burst opens a lag of about 107 ms and the playhead starts at about 1.2 times wall time. After the measured worst gap of 262 ms it starts at about 1.5 times. So at 60 Hz no paint through bursts moves more than about one and a half paint intervals of playback, which is no visible jump. A 1 s lowered delay closes in about 0.55 s (500 ms times ln 3) at a peak of 3 times wall time. A shorter constant catches up faster but lurches harder; a longer one glides but leaves a lowered offset late for longer.
- Paint-rate bound (D-02). While the target stands, the closed-form step moves the playhead the same distance whatever the paint rate. An arrival is seen at the first paint after it, and that paint's whole `dt` moves toward the new target, so two paint rates can see the same arrival up to one interval of the slower rate apart. Per arrival, the two playheads differ by at most the distance the playhead covers over one interval of the slower rate at the speed that arrival opens: that interval times one plus the lag over the time constant. At 30 Hz against 60 Hz that is about 40 ms after a 107 ms burst and about 51 ms after the 262 ms gap. The speed law falls as the playhead advances, so the difference never grows while no new arrival lands, and it is zero at the first paint where both stand at the target.
- Interpretation, AC4 with D-03. "Never passes the target" is read as never crossing it from at or behind it. When the delay grows, the target steps behind the playhead without the playhead moving; D-03 then has it keep moving, slower than wall time, never past the newest queued end, until the target catches up with it. If the owner instead wants it to stand still while ahead, the ahead side of the speed law becomes zero and nothing else changes.
- Remaining behavior under D-01, not planned for change. While behind, the playhead moves at least at wall time, so a lag of L ms closes within L ms of wall time. A target that then stands still longer than that holds the playhead at the target until the next arrival. Over a regular 107 ms burst cycle that hold is about 10 ms, under one 60 Hz paint. Over a 262 ms gap after one burst it is about 165 ms, which shows as a pause, followed by a glide rather than a jump. Avoiding that pause needs the playhead to keep a lag behind the target, which D-01 rules out; Task 5's hand-back is where the owner sees it.
