## Durable decisions

- D-01 (gate on the fix): the playhead's lag is set once, at the first paint after a restart, to the newest frame end less the delay, and moves only on wall `dt` after that; any correction after the anchor changes the owner's v2.0.0 D-01 rule ("wall speed, arrivals do not steer it; drift and stall handling only if a measurement shows them needed"), so no fix is planned until the live capture (AC1) is in and the owner has decided, and the decision lands in `docs/spec/timing.md` ("The two clocks", Rule 1) with the decision in the commit. Evidence: `hqptuner/static/model/gauges/playhead.js:99-123`, `hqptuner/static/store/meter/loop.js:118-130,191-203`, `docs/spec/timing.md:29,37`, `.planning/_archive-v2.0.0/5/CONTEXT.md:19`.
- D-02 (what the fix must deliver): the lag after a start is the same at every sample rate and does not depend on when the server's reader or the browser loop connected; it comes from the one shared playhead, never from per-rate offsets or a per-view correction. Both candidate causes, the opening rush and poll-phase connect timing, are measured and fixed. Evidence: owner answer 2026-11-13; `hqptuner/static/store/meter/delay.js:320-327`, `hqptuner/static/store/meter/playhead.js`, `hqptuner/static/store/meter/spectrogram.js:187,196`, `hqptuner/static/store/apodhistory.js:180`, `docs/spec/timing.md:39`, `hqptuner/engine/metering.py:45,252-265`, `hqptuner/config.py:129-132`.
- D-08 (owner decision 2026-11-14, replaces the start-lag decision plan): a fix aimed at the start rush alone is a patch over a systemic issue and is rejected. The phase writes general tests first that the spectrum and spectrogram are smooth, with no assumption about the cause; they fail against today's code (`docs/spec/testing.md` rule 8), and the fix follows to make them pass. Smooth means three behaviors. (1) Steady lag: the delay between what is on screen and what is playing stays the same through a whole track, within one metering frame, after every start, at every sample rate, however the data arrives. (2) Steady motion: each paint shows the moment of playback its own time calls for, with no freeze, jump ahead or step back except at a real break such as a stop or a seek. (3) Steady spectrogram: it scrolls at a constant rate in playback time, with no stalls and no catch-up bursts. The tests replay the Plan 1 capture `tests/support/fixtures/meter_start_capture.json` and made-up bursty and drifting streams through the app's own stream code. The capture shows two symptoms these cover: a lead of about 560 ms after each rate change, and a gain of about 446 ms over 170 s of steady play at 44100 with no change (`.planning/phases/7/CAPTURE.md`). The late-connect runs were cut by the owner, so poll-phase connect timing is unmeasured. AC2's decision step is dropped; any spec change the fix needs goes to the owner verbatim as usual. Evidence: owner answers 2026-11-14.
- D-09 (owner decisions 2026-11-14): (a) what is heard, in the capture replay, is HQPlayer's reported playback position (Status `position`), not the metering stream's own `at`/`n` sample count; the tests measure the visuals' lag against that position. (b) The D-08 tests are committed while they fail, before the fix lands; this authorizes red commits for those test files only, until the fix makes them pass. Evidence: owner answers 2026-11-14.
- D-10 (owner decision 2026-11-16, amends D-08 and D-09 (a)): the smoothness tests do not use a captured `position` fit as their clock. Status `position` moves in 106.67 ms steps, is read every 200 ms and its residuals are autocorrelated (lag-1 about 0.58 on every track), so a fit cannot resolve one metering frame on a short track. The two captures (`tests/support/fixtures/meter_start_capture.json`, `tests/support/fixtures/meter_start_capture_2.json`) are measured for how the data arrives: the opening rush at each rate (size and length), the gaps at track changes and the slow drift at 44100. Replay streams reproduce those measured patterns with a heard clock known exactly, and every steady-lag, steady-motion and steady-spectrogram comparison runs on them at one metering frame: across starts, across rates and across join points (AC3, AC4, AC5). Evidence: owner answers 2026-11-16; plan review findings on PLAN-2 (autocorrelated residuals; no cross-join comparison).
- D-11 (owner decision 2026-11-16, tightens D-08 (1) across rates): when a frame lands on screen does not depend on the source rate. A frame of a 44100 track and a frame of a 192000 track land at exactly the same moment relative to when they are heard: the lag after a start at one rate equals the lag at any other rate within floating-point error, not within a metering frame. Paints fall at the same wall times at every rate, so any difference is a rate leak into the playhead. 192000 is covered by a third live capture the owner makes, not by a made-up stream. The steady-motion suite (Plan 3 Task 2) stays as written although it is green against today's code: it pins a real contract, and the defect is lag, not motion. Evidence: owner answers 2026-11-16. [corrected by D-12: "any difference is a rate leak" holds only when the page holds the same information at both rates; on the captured starts each start's lead is estimated from arrivals seen at paints, so no rule can match them to float error]
- D-12 (owner decision 2026-11-16, amends D-11): D-11's exact cross-rate check is split into two contracts. (1) Rate-agnostic: paired replay streams identical in everything but the source rate (same arrival wall times, same playback ms per burst, same lead and delay, so the page holds the same information at both rates) give the same lag, within floating-point error; any difference is the rate's alone. (2) Each start's true lag: the comparisons across the captured starts hold within the resolution the stream's own paints and arrivals allow, a tolerance computed from that stream's known timing, never a round figure. The replays paint at 60 Hz, as a browser does. The heard clock stays the stamps' settled line less the output delay; the owner's ears are the check on that assumption, not a test. Evidence: owner answers 2026-11-16; Plan 4's design record section 3.
- D-13 (owner decision 2026-11-16, amends D-12 (2)): the page time-stamps each metering event the moment it arrives, so the playhead reads each arrival's own wall time, not the paint that first sees it. The resolution a stream allows therefore carries no paint term: it is the arrival grain plus the stream's jitter. That one computed tolerance holds for every comparison between two starts, at the same rate or at different rates (about 1 ms on the captured streams); the one-metering-frame tolerance for same-rate comparisons is retired. The 17.667 ms figure came from the proposed rule reading arrivals at paints, and a tolerance derived from the design's own limit is a fudge. Evidence: owner answers 2026-11-16.
- D-14 (owner decision 2026-11-17, supersedes the tolerances of D-10, D-11, D-12 (2) and D-13): the smoothness tests are binary, per `docs/spec/testing.md` rule 18. No check compares a lag, a move or a scroll against an allowance of any kind: not one metering frame, not a stream's resolution, not a perception figure, not floating-point slack. Each test feeds input whose right answer is known exactly, every time in whole samples and whole ms, and asserts exact equality: after a start the playhead stands one delay behind the music on every paint, as it does later in the track; two streams identical but for the source rate give identical playheads at every paint; and each promise of the approved rule (never backward outside a break, never past the newest frame, a restart at a break) holds as a yes or no. The steady-lag, steady-motion and steady-spectrogram suites of Plans 2 to 4, and their replay streams, are rewritten to this; their tolerance checks are deleted. Cause: Plan 5 Task 4 found 22 steady-lag cases needing the playhead to beat its own input (a one-frame bound on streams built with 40 ms jitter), and the captured replay streams were rebuilt from a clean clock (scatter 0.5 ms against 73 to 91 ms in the raw captures), so the suites measured neither reality nor a coherent rule. Evidence: owner answers 2026-11-17; Plan 5 report.

## Decisions

- D-03 (stamps): frame stamps are a server-side count of metered samples from the moment the reader opened port 4322 (`_fed += bins - 1`, reset on connect), not the engine's playback position; stamp 0 is the connect moment. Evidence: `hqptuner/engine/meterfeed.py:197-222`, `hqptuner/engine/metering.py:40,278-279`, `docs/spec/protocol.md:403-406`.
- D-04 (restarts): the anchor restarts on the browser loop starting, a server reconnect (State leaves playing, a subscriber comes or goes), a geometry change (every rate change), and a dropped event (`QUEUE_DEPTH = 8`, partial stride); a gapless same-rate track change restarts nothing as far as the code shows, and the capture measures whether every track start re-anchors. Evidence: `hqptuner/static/store/meter/feed.js:108-121`, `hqptuner/engine/meterfeed.py:83,111-113,203-230`, `hqptuner/engine/metering.py:243-297`, `hqptuner/static/store/meter/loop.js:229-258`; owner answer 2026-11-13 ("don't know, measure it").
- D-05 (capture): a new direct capture off port 4322, run by the `meter-probe` agent, records each frame's arrival wall time next to its `at`/`n` stamps, the geometry events, and Status position, track serial and `output_delay` sampled across the window; it starts before a track starts and covers a same-rate change and a rate change at two rates, with the owner starting the tracks. It writes nothing to the daemon, so no idle gate applies; it is wire-protocol measurement under the probing rule. The existing `scripts/probes/capture_meter_feed.py` (through `:8090`, after the container's reader connects, `output_delay` read once) and `scripts/probes/probe_metering_stream.py` (no per-frame arrival times) are not used as they stand. Evidence: owner answer 2026-11-13; `scripts/probes/capture_meter_feed.py:1-23,48-58,108-120`, `scripts/probes/probe_metering_stream.py:1-75`, `.claude/agents/meter-probe.md:16-36`.
- D-06 (tests pinning today's behavior): a fix that moves the anchor or adds a correction rewrites these, each rewrite red against the old code: `tests/js/model/gauges/playhead.test.js:477,593-599,609,630,680` and `tests/js/store/meter/loop.test.js:166`; no test covers a feed that starts faster than real time. Evidence: those files; `docs/spec/testing.md` rule 8.
- D-07 (nothing closes lag today): the v2.0.0 UAT line "playhead closes lag after a long burst gap" tested the target-tracking design that `c89ed07a` deleted with `CATCH_UP_MS`; the PROJECT.md key decision "capped at the newest stamp less the delay" is stale the same way, since the cap is now the newest end. Evidence: `.planning/_archive-v2.0.0/5/UAT.md:11-17`, `hqptuner/static/model/gauges/playhead.js:5-11`, `.planning/PROJECT.md:76`.

## Flagged assumptions

- Re-anchoring during the rush may come mostly from queue-overflow breaks (`QUEUE_DEPTH = 8`) rather than the geometry break - Unclear; if wrong: the fix's trigger set is wrong; AC1's capture shows which.
- Where hqplayerd taps metering relative to output, and whether it runs faster than real time at a start - unresearched, no repo doc; the live capture and the hqpdoc tools answer it.
- What Status `position` and `output_delay` mean (heard or processed position; whether `output_delay` holds steady across a start) - unresearched; `docs/spec/protocol.md:299-300` lists the fields without semantics.
