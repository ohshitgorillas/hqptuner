WHAT IS WRONG

junkrules made the detector stateless in the rules and per-bin in the hold: a spectrum is classified on its own, and a spur bin is held until its excess falls under `SPUR_RELEASE_DB` or the bin stops standing clear of the floor (`hqptuner/engine/junkadvisor.py:135-147`, `SpurHolder.decide`). Three sites in the reader still impose the older model on top of those rules.

1. Readiness. `SpectralAggregate.window_min_db` returns None until six full five-second blocks exist (`hqptuner/engine/metering.py:166-167`, `if len(self._blocks) < WINDOW_BLOCKS: return None`), so no verdict of any kind is possible for the first ~30 s of a stream. `hqptuner/engine/junkadvisor.py:161` calls it "the only readiness gate there is". A tone the rules can read off the first block is not read for half a minute.

2. Track identity in the read. `verdict()` returns None whenever the engine's `track_serial` differs from the one the aggregate was built under (`hqptuner/engine/metering.py:233`), so the advice drops on the first poll after a track change even though the evidence in hand is unchanged.

3. Track identity in the write. `_ingest` rebuilds the aggregate, resets `self._serial` and throws away the spur hold whenever `ctx.track_serial` changes (`hqptuner/engine/metering.py:346-349`), so every track change costs a fresh ~30 s readiness wait on top of the hold.

Together these are the "wait, window and track identity" the junkrules rules say the verdict does not read. The hold is keyed by frequency rather than bin index precisely so a re-grid carries it (`hqptuner/engine/junkadvisor.py:126-128`), and the release rule is already the one the owner wants: a filter is never held where the spur is not visible.
