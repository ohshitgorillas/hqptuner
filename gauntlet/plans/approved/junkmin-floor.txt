What is wrong

The advisor computes one floor for the whole track and one curve for three rules. classify smooths the mean spectrum and takes a grid-wide 10th percentile of it (junkadvisor.py:115-116), then hands that single floor to the brick-wall rule and the ramp rule (junkadvisor.py:118-120). The spur rule already reads a different curve and so has to compute its own floor, which it does by repeating the same grid-wide 10th percentile on the minimum curve (junkadvisor.py:271).

That percentile is frequency-blind, and on a minimum curve the floor is strongly frequency-dependent. Two mechanisms push the grid-wide statistic far below the floor of the band any rule actually reads:

- Empty bins are clamped, not measured. metering.py:178 maps a zero power bin to -200.0 dB, and for a source upsampled into a wider container the whole region above the source Nyquist is exactly that. In a 176.4 kHz container carrying content to 22 kHz, well over 10% of the grid sits at -200.0, so the 10th percentile is -200.0 outright.
- Where the region is not clamped but carries real dither, a per-bin minimum over a window of frames sits far below the same bin's mean, because it selects the quietest frame rather than averaging. The owner's ~40 dB figure across /srv/hqptuner/state/junkcal/*.jsonl is that offset. ASSUMED: the magnitude is the owner's measurement and settling it myself needs a metered run over the corpus (section E); the direction and the mechanism are verified above.

Either way the floor lands far below the level of the 18-26 kHz band, so `floor + ABOVE_FLOOR_DB` (junkadvisor.py:200) sits under every bin in the window, every bin reads as content, `edge` reaches the window's top bin, and _content_edge returns None on its own "a ceiling at the top bin is not a ceiling" guard (junkadvisor.py:205). _at_floor_above (junkadvisor.py:210) and the ramp's `top < floor + RAMP_ABOVE_FLOOR_DB` (junkadvisor.py:298) go vacuous by the same arithmetic. This is why the floor cannot simply be rehomed along with the rules.

The existing test fixtures cannot show any of this. tests/support/junk_spectra.py:21-53 builds flat synthetic floors at -110 or -140 dB across the whole grid, with no clamped stopband and no minimum-of-N depression, so a grid-wide percentile lands on the intended floor there and nowhere else.

A. Rehoming the two rules

_ceiling_wall (junkadvisor.py:222-234) and _ramp (junkadvisor.py:292-303) read the minimum curve instead of the mean. Both signatures are claims about absence — content that never appears above a cutoff, noise that is present in every frame — and a minimum is the curve that carries absence. The gain is concrete: for an upsampled source the region above the source Nyquist is a hard -200.0 on the minimum curve rather than a smeared mean, so the 30 dB drop test (junkadvisor.py:232) gets easier to satisfy for a genuine fake-hires master, while a genuine hi-res track whose 18-26 kHz content is intermittent sinks to the floor and yields no edge at all, which is the conservative answer.

The accepted cost is inside _ceiling_wall: its 15-18 kHz reference band (junkadvisor.py:231) is a presence reading, and on a minimum curve a quiet passage inside the window sinks it. Both sides of that comparison stay on the minimum curve rather than straddling two curves, because a dB difference between a mean level and a minimum level is not a difference in a single unit and would inflate the drop by the depression offset alone. The consequence is a false negative on a track that goes quiet in 15-18 kHz across the whole window, not a false positive, and section C shortens the window early in a track, which reduces it.

_ramp is unaffected by that objection: noise-shaping noise is broadband and present in every frame, so the minimum carries it, while a loud passage's transients can no longer inflate the mean's top band.

This overturns a rule currently in force. junkadvisor.py:22-27 states that the brick-wall rule reads the mean "only through statistics a passage cannot shift far: the highest bin standing clear of the noise floor, and two band means at fixed frequencies", and that "A local slope on the same curve answers one way in a loud passage and another in a quiet one, for the same master." The reason those statistics were chosen was that the mean keeps moving as a track plays.

Section A retires that reasoning for _ceiling_wall and _ramp, and it does not replace it with an equivalent guarantee. What section C proves is narrower: the minimum over the last six blocks is stationary with track length, so a reading taken at minute eight is drawn from the same amount of evidence as one taken at minute one. Passage-stability itself is not preserved — a minimum over the last six blocks is a reading of the last thirty seconds of passage, and a quiet passage inside that window sinks the reference band, which is the cost already named above and accepted there as a false negative. The exchange is deliberate: the statistics the docstring defends were protecting a curve that smears absence, and absence is what these two rules claim. With section D's excision the mean has no reader left at all, so the rule the docstring states survives for nothing: the docstring is rewritten to say what each of the three rules now reads, and it is in the touched list.

B. A floor statistic that does not collapse

The floor stops being one grid-wide number computed in classify and becomes a band-local low percentile of the minimum curve, taken per reading. Which band each reading takes it from is part of the design and is stated here rather than left to section E, because a band mean compared against a low percentile of its own bins passes for any band that is flat in its own terms, however loud — the same vacuity the brief is about, moved to a new place. The rule is that the level under test may not be what sets the floor it is tested against, and each of the three readings earns that differently:

- _content_edge and _at_floor_above (junkadvisor.py:193-210) share one floor, the low percentile of BRICK_WINDOW_HZ. That window straddles the ceiling whenever the rule has anything to say, so its low bins are the empty region above the ceiling and its high bins are content. _at_floor_above's tested band runs from edge + guard to the top bin and so overlaps the window only above the ceiling; the case where that overlap would be degenerate — a uniformly loud band passing floor + ABOVE_FLOOR_DB against its own percentile — cannot reach it, because a flat window is exactly the window in which no bin stands ABOVE_FLOOR_DB above the percentile and _content_edge has already returned None (junkadvisor.py:200-205).
- _ramp's energy gate (junkadvisor.py:298) takes its floor from RAMP_LO_HZ to the top bin, the whole region the rule reads, not from the top band it tests. The same ordering does the work: the gate is reached only after rise >= RAMP_RISE_DB (junkadvisor.py:297-298), which is the statement that the region is not flat, so its low percentile sits in the lower HF band and not in the top one. A flat region — floor wobble, the thing the gate exists to reject — fails the rise test first.
- _spurs (junkadvisor.py:271) takes its floor from its own hunt band, SPUR_MIN_HZ to the top bin. Here the tested value is one bin's raw level, not a band mean, and the band is hundreds of bins wide, so a tone a few bins wide cannot move the percentile it is measured against. This one is non-degenerate by width rather than by ordering.

Both ordering arguments are properties of the code as it stands after this change, readable in it, and neither depends on section E's numbers.

The spur line is the one the brief cites by number. Its floor + 8 gate at junkadvisor.py:275 stops reading a grid-wide statistic that the clamped stopband above the source Nyquist drags below every bin it gates. It is a floor change, and a floor change moves the firing set: junkadvisor.py:275 gates each candidate on floor + ABOVE_FLOOR_DB, so a band-local floor admits and rejects different tones than a grid-wide one, and section E measures which. What does not change is the code around it — the curve the rule reads, its wide-median baseline, its 15 dB excess test, the corner split and the verdict it returns — and latching, which the brief puts out of scope.

The band-local shape fails in the right directions in all three cases a grid-wide percentile confuses: a band that is part content and part empty puts the percentile in the empty part, which is the floor the rule wants; a band that is entirely content puts it at the content level so nothing clears floor + 8 and no ceiling is claimed; a band that is entirely empty does the same. It is also the only shape the capture corpus can validate, because the rows store the curve from 13 kHz up only (junkcal.py:45), and carry no full grid.

The percentile value, and whether ABOVE_FLOOR_DB and RAMP_ABOVE_FLOOR_DB still hold at 8 and 20 dB on a minimum curve, are set by the corpus run in section E and land as module constants. The bands above are not section E's to set: they are the design, and the run measures the thresholds read against them. Every rule now reads a minimum curve, so there is no second curve to keep constants apart from and the three rules share one set. ASSUMED until that run: that the present 8 and 20 dB values do not survive unchanged.

C. Ungating the minimum curve

SpectralAggregate gains min_db(): the same per-bin minimum window_min_db() already computes, with the six-block length check lifted off it. It is a trailing window, not a whole-track minimum, because self._blocks is already a deque(maxlen=WINDOW_BLOCKS) (metering.py:135), so the minimum spans the last six full blocks plus the partial one however long the track runs. It returns None only before the first non-silent frame has been folded into a block. window_min_db() becomes min_db() behind the unchanged length check (metering.py:172), and the spur rule remains its only reader, because that gate is the spur rule's persistence proof — a tone that survives six blocks of minima cannot be a sustained musical partial. classify takes both curves, ungated and gated; _spurs keeps reading the gated one, _ceiling_wall and _ramp read the ungated one.

Bounded rather than monotone matters twice. The curve does not deepen without limit as a track plays, so _ceiling_wall's 15-18 kHz reference reading, the band-local floor under it and their difference (junkadvisor.py:232) are stationary after the first six blocks rather than drifting apart; a minimum over every block ever seen would have sunk both readings for the length of the track, and by different amounts. It also makes the capture corpus the right instrument for section E, since the rows store exactly this window once it is earned.

Before the window is earned the curve is shallower, because fewer blocks have been minimised together. Both the band-local floor and the content above it rise together, so what shrinks is the contrast between them, and the rules fire less readily early in a track than late. That is the conservative direction, and it is also the residual the corpus cannot measure: the constants from section E are fixed against a fully-earned window, and ASSUMED to hold on the shorter one, which is where a false negative, never a false positive, would live.

What neither rule may do is read a curve that is not there. Silent frames buy coverage but never enter the minimum (metering.py:149-155, pinned at tests/engine/test_junk_persistence.py:97 and :106), so a track that opens on digital silence can satisfy eligible()'s MIN_SECONDS with min_db() still None. With the mean gone (section D) there is nothing to fall through to, so classify returns None outright in that case, which is the same thing the user sees today for such a track: no note until there is a curve to read.

What the user sees: no change in when a 20k or 50k note appears relative to today. Today those rules read the mean and are behind eligible()'s MIN_SECONDS alone (junkadvisor.py:113-120), never behind the persistence window. Section A would have put them behind it, adding a wait for BLOCK_SECONDS x WINDOW_BLOCKS (30 s, metering.py:57-58) to be earned on top of the 15 s; section C is what keeps that cost from being incurred. What does change is which tracks earn a note at all, which is sections A and D's subject and section E's measurement. Spur notes keep their 30 s wait.

D. _lossy_wall is excised, and a 20k verdict must be able to cut something

_lossy_wall (junkadvisor.py:237-248) is deleted, not rehomed and not kept on the mean.

The reason is that its verdict is inert wherever it fires. The rule finds a content ceiling inside LOSSY_WINDOW_HZ = (13_000.0, 18_000.0) (junkadvisor.py:72, :239), requires a BRICK_DROP_DB fall across the ceiling (junkadvisor.py:244), and then requires _at_floor_above(smoothed, edge + guard, floor) (junkadvisor.py:246): the band mean from one 1.5 kHz guard above the ceiling to the top bin sits within ABOVE_FLOOR_DB of the floor. The ceiling is at most 18 kHz, so that already-empty band begins at most 19.5 kHz and runs to the source Nyquist. The verdict it returns recommends the 20k corner (junkadvisor.py:213-219), which removes content above 20 kHz — entirely inside the band the rule has just established is at the floor. A user who engages it hears nothing change. The owner's ruling is the general form: if the 20k filter does not actually resolve the issue, we do not recommend it.

That also answers the brief's question of whether the rule survives on a minimum curve. It does not need to: the objection the brief raises — a minimum reading intermittent 13-18 kHz sibilance as a cutoff, producing a 30 dB step the master does not have — would make it fire more often, and every extra firing is one more recommendation that cuts nothing. The rule is removed rather than moved.

The corner refusal. The same arithmetic reaches _ceiling_wall from below. Its window starts at 18 kHz (BRICK_WINDOW_HZ, junkadvisor.py:63), so a ceiling it reports can sit anywhere in 18-26 kHz, and a ceiling below 20 kHz gets the same inert 20k recommendation by the same _at_floor_above evidence (junkadvisor.py:228). Per the owner's ruling, _ceiling_wall returns None where the detected ceiling is below the 20 kHz corner. The threshold is read from the corner table already in the module (_CORNER_KHZ["20k"], junkadvisor.py:139) rather than typed as a second copy of 20 kHz. Raising BRICK_WINDOW_HZ[0] to 20 kHz would produce the identical firing set — _content_edge takes the highest bin above the limit, so an edge in 18-20 kHz becomes no edge at all when the scan starts at 20 kHz — and the explicit refusal is chosen over it because it states the rule where a reader will look for it, and leaves the window meaning what it says.

What the user sees. Fewer 20k notes, in two cases and no others: a source cut in 13-18 kHz (a lossy master upsampled into a hi-res container) now earns no note, and a source whose ceiling sits in 18-20 kHz now earns none either. Both cases today produce a note recommending a filter that removes nothing. Everything at or above 20 kHz is unchanged; the note's wording is unchanged; 30k, 40k and 50k verdicts are untouched. Auto-pilot engages the 20k filter in exactly the cases the note names (lanes/autopilot.py:31-33), so it stops engaging it in those two. It is a user-visible behavior change and a CHANGELOG entry is owed with it at stage 2, written and approved there before implementation.

No user-facing string is deleted. The reason copy is built in _fake_hires (junkadvisor.py:213-219), which _ceiling_wall shares and keeps; _lossy_wall has no copy of its own, nothing in hqptuner/data/ or the frontend switches on the verdict's "20k" token, and AlertStrip.js:45 renders advice.reason verbatim. So the copy owed at stage 2 is the changelog line alone, and the deletions are non-user-facing prose: the module docstring's account of the rules (junkadvisor.py:22-35), junkcal.py's docstring line naming "the lossy window's floor" (junkcal.py:9, :41) and scripts/junkcal_view.py:4-10.

What the excision takes with it. _lossy_wall was the last reader of the mean spectrum, so the mean goes:

- LOSSY_WINDOW_HZ (junkadvisor.py:70-72) is deleted.
- classify's levels_db parameter (junkadvisor.py:95, :105) is deleted; classify takes the ungated minimum curve in its place, plus the gated one for _spurs (section C). The grid-wide floor computed at junkadvisor.py:115-116 goes with it, replaced per reading by section B's band-local helper.
- eligible()'s bins argument (junkadvisor.py:113, :126) is taken from the minimum curve's length instead of the mean's. Both curves are full-grid arrays of the aggregate's bins (metering.py:165, :177-178), so the count is the same number; only where it is read from changes. eligible()'s other call site (junkcal.py:57) passes agg.bins directly and is unaffected.
- SpectralAggregate.levels_db() and the _power sums behind it (metering.py:134, :145-146, :163-165) are deleted. self.frames stays: it is the guard at metering.py:235 and a captured row field (junkcal.py:68), and _frame_power (metering.py:384) still feeds the block minimum.
- _brick_wall (junkadvisor.py:251-255) loses its second arm and becomes the FAKE_HIRES_MIN_RATE gate in front of _ceiling_wall alone.

junkcal.py:45 takes the capture's lower frequency bound from LOSSY_WINDOW_HZ[0]. The capture format must not change, so the 13 kHz bound moves rather than vanishing: junkcal.py gains a constant of its own at that value, which is what it has always meant there — the lowest frequency the calibration corpus stores, not a signature window. The stored rows keep the same lowest bin (13031.25 Hz on a 48 kHz-bandwidth 1025-bin frame, 13006.0546875 Hz on an 88.2 kHz one, measured at tests/specs/junkcal-a4a6.txt:26), so existing capture files and the new ones stay one corpus and section E reads them together.

Rule order

classify's or-chain keeps its three arms in their order: _brick_wall, then _spurs, then _ramp (junkadvisor.py:117-120). _brick_wall is now single-armed. The first arm wins, so widening what it fires on can take a track that reaches _spurs today and answer it with a 20k verdict instead, which then latches for the rest of the track (metering.py:248-250).

The geometry where the two arms can compete is narrow: a persistent tone between SPUR_MIN_HZ at 25 kHz and BRICK_WINDOW_HZ's top at 26 kHz, which is the only overlap the two windows have. A tone there does not produce a ceiling, because _content_edge reads the 9-bin median-smoothed curve while a spur is a few bins wide — the same erasure that forces _spurs to hunt in raw values and to compute its own wide baseline (junkadvisor.py:261-266). What remains is a source that both stops inside 18-26 kHz and carries a tone above the stop, and that track is answered by _brick_wall first today as well, on the same or-chain; section A makes it likelier to be answered at all rather than falling through with no verdict. The user-visible cost of the short-circuit is the reason copy naming fake hi-res rather than a tone, not a wrong action: the recommended 20k corner is at or below the 30k a spur verdict would have asked for, so treated() counts it as treatment of the tone too (junkadvisor.py:139/153). The corner refusal cannot make that worse — it only turns 20k verdicts into no verdict, which lets the chain fall through to _spurs and _ramp on tracks that today short-circuit at a ceiling below 20 kHz.
