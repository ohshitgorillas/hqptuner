what is wrong

The detector has a 30 s gate in front of it and no rule needs one.

`window_min_db()` returns None until `WINDOW_BLOCKS` full blocks exist (`metering.py:166-167`), blocks are `BLOCK_SECONDS` of coverage each (`metering.py:58-59`), so 6 × 5 s = 30 s of covered playback before any verdict of any kind. A block that saw only silent frames is dropped instead of pushed (`metering.py:156-159`), and silence is any frame whose every channel RMS sits below `SILENT_RMS_DB` (`metering.py:63`, read at `metering.py:364`), so a quiet intro pays coverage into blocks that never arrive and pushes the first verdict out past 30 s.

`classify` names that gate as the only one left (`junkadvisor.py:99-101`), which is true and is the defect: the input is a 30 s rolling minimum, so the verdict is a property of the last 30 s rather than of what is playing. It is the same time-averaging the ruling at `docs/gauntlet/plans/approved/junk-live-verdict.txt:12` removed from the mean spectrum, kept because that plan's brief named `window_min_db()` as the input to write the rules against.

Two further statements are stale in the tree and are corrected here, since this change rewrites the lines they sit on.

- `CHANGELOG.md:13`, unreleased, says cutoffs "as low as 13 kHz are recognized". `LOSSY_WINDOW_HZ` is gone and `BRICK_WINDOW_HZ` is `(20_000.0, 26_000.0)` (`junkadvisor.py:62`), so nothing below 20 kHz is recognized. Change 7 of the approved plan called for this line to be replaced and it did not land.
- `scripts/junkcal_view.py:8-10` says the brick-wall and ramp rules read a mean spectrum that is not captured, so a recorded 20k or 50k verdict cannot be recomputed from its row. All three rules have read the windowed minimum since the last change, so the sentence is wrong twice over.

what changes

1. `hqptuner/engine/metering.py`

1a. `SpectralAggregate` holds one hop instead of a window. Deleted: `self._blocks`, `self._block_min`, `self._block_seconds` (`:135-137`), the block fold and push in `add` (`:146-159`), `window_min_db` (`:161-176`), `BLOCK_SECONDS` and `WINDOW_BLOCKS` (`:58-59`) with the comment above them (`:51-57`). Added: `self._latest: list[float] | None`, which `add` sets to a copy of the frame it is given. New accessor `frame_db()` returns that frame in dB by the same conversion `window_min_db` used (`10 * log10(p)`, `-200.0` at zero), or None before the first frame of the current track. `self.frames` and `self.seconds` stay: junkcal writes both into its rows (`junkcal.py:69-70`) and `scripts/junkcal_view.py:237` reads `seconds`.

The accessor is named `frame_db`, not `levels_db`: `levels_db` was the mean accessor deleted by the previous change, and the tests that read it were rewritten then. A new meaning on the old name would read as a revert.

The class keeps its name. It no longer aggregates a spectrum, but `frames` and `seconds` are still per-track counters the capture reads, and a rename moves `junkcal.py:32`, `metering.py` and every test that builds one for no behaviour.

1b. Silence handling stays and changes shape. `SILENT_RMS_DB` (`:63`), `_frame_silent` (`:358-364`), the `silent` keyword of `add` (`:139`) and its call-site argument (`:345`) are all kept. What `add` does with the flag changes: instead of holding the frame out of a minimum, it stores no hop at all for a silent frame (`self._latest = None`), so `frame_db()` returns None and a silent hop earns no verdict. That is not memory and not a readiness gate — nothing accumulates and nothing is withheld while it does; a hop with nothing audible in it has nothing to say, and the next audible hop says whatever it says.

The alternative — delete the guard with the minimum it was written for — was considered and rejected, because the two ends of the threshold do not behave alike without it. Digital silence is all-zero power, which the dB conversion sends to `-200.0` in every bin: a flat curve whose own 10th-percentile floor is the curve itself, so nothing clears floor + 8 dB and no rule fires. A frame just under the threshold is not that. Every channel's RMS below -90 dBFS still leaves an ordinary quiet curve with structure in it, and `_spurs` measures excess against that same curve's own 51-bin baseline and its own floor (`junkadvisor.py:247-252`), both of which sink with it — so a narrow tone in near-silence clears 15 dB of excess just as it would at level. Deleting the guard would therefore hand the spur rule a new false-firing region at the bottom of the scale, which is the region inter-track hiss lives in. Keeping it puts one sharp line at -90 dBFS instead. The line itself was set for the minimum and is not retuned here, for the reason 2b gives.

1c. `verdict` (`:215-234`) returns None unless `ctx.playing`. Today a pause deliberately keeps the aggregate standing (`:259`, `run` at `:271`), which was right for a window earned over the track; a held hop is a picture of a moment that has stopped. The serial guard at `:228` stays as it is. The aggregate itself still survives the pause, so the capture's counters and grid do not restart on a pause.

This one has a consequence outside the reader, and it is a behaviour change in its own right. With auto-pilot on, a None verdict resolves to `NO_FILTER` (`lanes/autopilot.py:31-32`), and `_move` compares that against the engaged filter without consulting `ctx.playing` (`core/autopilotops.py:57-61`) before writing through the LIVE lane (`core/autopilotops.py:81`). So 1c makes a pause release the engaged junk filter and the resume re-engage it: two audited daemon writes per pause, where today the verdict survives the pause (`metering.py:259`) and the filter stays put — behaviour pinned at `tests/engine/test_metering_idle.py:169`, which this change reverses.

Releasing on pause is what I think is wanted, and it is my call rather than a question: nothing is playing, the ruling is engage when the signature is present and release when it is absent (`docs/gauntlet/plans/approved/junk-live-verdict.txt:14`), and a filter held across a pause is held on the strength of a hop from before it. Two writes per pause is also small beside the 2 s ceiling priced below — a pause is a user action, not a loop. If you would rather a pause hold the filter, that is one word and 1c drops the `ctx.playing` test, at the cost of the reader reporting a signature for audio that stopped.

1d. `frame_db()` replaces `window_min_db()` at the one production read, `verdict` (`:231`).

1e. Comments and docstrings rewritten, no behaviour: the `DECIMATE` comment (`:45-47`), which explains the value by minima folded over seconds of coverage — the value 4 stays, and what it now means is that the hop the rules read is at most four hops old (≤ 93 ms at 44.1 kHz, ≤ 43 ms at 96 kHz, from ~43 hops/s at 44.1 kHz, `protocol.md:289`); the module docstring's aggregate and verdict-lifetime paragraphs (`:1-19`); the `SpectralAggregate` class docstring (`:119-126`); `add` (`:139-144`); `verdict` (`:216`); `recommendation` (`:237`); and `run`'s "a pause mid-track leaves the evidence standing, so the verdict survives it" (`:259`), which 1c overturns.

what each rule reads, and what it costs

All three now read one transform hop, ~21 ms of audio at 96 kHz. What that does to each is not the same, and the answer is not symmetric.

20k, brick wall. Reads the 9-bin median of the hop. True positives survive intact: a brick-walled master is brick-walled in every hop, so the ceiling and the empty band above it are there whenever the rule looks. The cost is on the other side of the test. `BRICK_DROP_DB` compares the fixed 15–18 kHz reference band against the band above the ceiling (`junkadvisor.py:228-231`), and on a minimum curve both ends were 30 s minima, so a quiet moment anywhere in the window pulled the reference down and the drop shrank. In one hop the reference band is whatever is sounding at that instant. Any hop with strong 15–18 kHz content — a cymbal, sibilance, a hi-hat — and nothing above 22 kHz clears 30 dB on an authentic hi-res master that the windowed minimum would have refused. Expect the rule to fire on genuine hi-res during HF-heavy transients and fall silent between them, which is false firing and flapping from the same cause.

30k/40k, spurs. Reads the raw per-bin values of the hop against a 51-bin median of the same hop. This rule loses its entire discriminator. "Persistent" was never a test in the rule body; it was a property of the input, enforced by the 30 s minimum, and the module docstring's field case says so directly — a 30.3 kHz tone 15 dB proud during a quiet intro fell to 6 dB of excess once the music started (`junkadvisor.py:30-32`). Against a hop, "narrow peak ≥ `SPUR_DB` above the local median and above floor + 8 dB" is satisfied by a bias tone, by a cymbal partial, by transient ringing, by any narrow HF feature that happens to be sounding in that 21 ms. The field case inverts: the tone now shows during the quiet intro and disappears under the music, rather than the reverse. This is the noisiest of the three by a wide margin and the one whose copy stops being true (2d).

50k, noise-shaping ramp. Reads the same smoothed hop. True positives survive for the same reason as 20k — shaping is in every hop. False firing barely moves: music energy falls toward Nyquist and a hop that rises toward it is still unusual. What it gains instead is drop-out: `RAMP_ABOVE_FLOOR_DB` requires the top band to sit 20 dB above the floor (`junkadvisor.py:277`), and in a single quiet hop it does not, so the verdict vanishes and returns with the next loud one. Flicker, not false firing.

Common to all three, and the largest cost in the change: the verdict now changes as fast as anything reads it. Auto-pilot reads every `poll_interval`, 2.0 s by default (`config.py:81`, passed at `api/lifespan.py:78`), and writes the junk filter to the production hqplayerd whenever the answer differs from what is engaged. The ceiling is therefore one LIVE-lane write every 2 s, on the order of 1 800 an hour, against a daemon this repo treats as live production. The writes are the ordinary audited lane and a junk-filter change costs the stream nothing (manual §2.8, recorded at `docs/architecture.md:99`), so this is load and audit volume rather than an interruption — but it is a change of kind, not of degree, from a latched verdict that moved 258 times in 8.6 hours. Hysteresis and a hold timer are ruled out by `docs/gauntlet/plans/approved/junk-live-verdict.txt:14`, so the plan discloses this rather than damping it. If you want it damped, that ruling is the one to revise, and it is a separate change.

Against all of that, the thing the brief is buying: first advice arrives a second or two into playback instead of 30 s in, and a quiet intro no longer pushes it out further. The figure is not the hop age. The connect path this plan leaves in place is the floor: `ctx.playing` is the manager's last status poll (`metering.py:79-80`, `:86`) at `poll_interval` 2.0 s (`config.py:81`), the reader re-checks the idle gate every `IDLE_RECHECK` = 1.0 s (`metering.py:44`), and only then is a hop ingested and decimated (≤ 93 ms at 44.1 kHz). One poll staleness plus one recheck plus one decimated hop is what a listener waits, against the same path plus 30 s of covered non-silent playback today.

evidence

There is none, and no honest way to manufacture it.

The 20 capture files under `/srv/hqptuner/state/junkcal` store the windowed minimum (`junkcal.py:43-49`), which is the input this change deletes. Running the proposed rules over those curves would measure the old input under a new name and produce numbers that look like evidence and are not. The firing counts in `docs/gauntlet/plans/approved/junk-live-verdict.txt` are of that kind for the previous change and do not carry over here.

No corpus of per-hop spectra exists anywhere in the tree or on the host. Building one means capturing from the live metering stream against real playback over hours, which is the thing change 3 makes the capture do from the moment it lands.

So the false-firing section above is reasoning from the rule bodies and the input geometry, not measurement, and every quantity in it is a direction rather than a rate. The one number in it that is measured — 258 verdict changes over 8.6 hours — is the old detector's, quoted as the baseline this departs from. ASSUMED, and settling it needs the live capture the change itself creates: that the 20k and spur rules fire materially more often on authentic material than they do today.

That is also why 2b moves no threshold. Retuning `BRICK_DROP_DB`, `SPUR_DB` or `RAMP_RISE_DB` for a hop is a real need and there is nothing to set them from until the new capture has run.

cost

`make check` and a container rebuild through `/task-check`, then a hand-back URL. The advisor chip changes behaviour a user can see — it appears within a second or two of playback rather than half a minute in, and it now comes and goes with the music — so this takes an independent `user-reviewer` pass on the rebuilt container before hand-back, and your eyes after that.

No playback interruption, and the change writes nothing to the daemon by itself. What it changes is how often auto-pilot does: up to one LIVE-lane write every 2 s while playing, priced above, plus two per pause from 1c where today a pause costs none. All of them are the ordinary audited lane.

Two owner approvals are owed at stage 2 and named now so they are not a surprise: the corrected spur reason string (2d) and the corrected `README.md:200` wording (6), both alongside the changelog entry (7).

Risk sits in one place. The detector gets faster and less discriminating in the same stroke, thresholds that were set for a 30 s minimum stay where they are because nothing exists to move them to, and the first real measurement of the result is the capture this change starts writing.
