THE SPUR HOLD

Per-bin hysteresis on excess, never on level, per the owner's ruling. The holder carries a set of frequencies, each one a bin that has crossed the engage split and has not yet been released. It carries no level and no track identity.

On each window every held bin that no longer stands 11.0 dB clear of the current floor is released first, whatever its excess: a bin the rules cannot see is not a bin the verdict rests on, which is the owner's "a filter is never held where the spur is not visible". Then, for every bin above 25 kHz standing 11.0 dB clear of the floor, the excess over the current 51-bin baseline is computed, and each bin is decided on its own:
- at or over 22.0 dB the bin enters the held set, or stays in it;
- under 10.0 dB, the release split of THE RELEASE SPLIT below, it leaves the held set;
- between the two it stays as it was, held if it was held and absent if it was not.
The spur branch's verdict is then the lowest corner among the held bins, each bin's corner read from its own frequency against the 45 kHz split. An empty held set is no spur verdict.

The verdict a held set produces still carries one frequency, not a set. `reason` names one tone in kHz and `ceiling_khz` is one number (`hqptuner/engine/junkadvisor.py:257-266`), and neither changes shape. What constrains them is the `/api/status` payload rather than a UI read: `hqptuner/static/store/alerts/junkadvice.js:10` passes the whole `junk` object through untouched, `ceiling_khz` appears in no file under `hqptuner/static` at all (`grep -rn "ceiling_khz" hqptuner/static`, no hits), and the only field the frontend names is `reason`, rendered at `hqptuner/static/components/AlertStrip.js:45` and asserted at `tests/js/components/chrome/junkadvice.test.js:69`. Both are taken from the bin that decided the verdict: the held bin of the winning corner, and where several held bins share that corner, the one with the greatest current excess. A held bin of a higher corner is not named while a lower one is winning. A baseline rising over a spur therefore releases that bin, which is the case a level-based hold would have got wrong, and a bin the spur has left releases whatever the other bins do.

- Two bins implying different corners: the lower corner wins while both are held, and the verdict rises to the higher corner by itself when the lower bin releases. No bin's release is conditional on another's.

THE RAMP RULE

Two constants, both read off the fixture corpus, one structural fact that decides how much they have to carry, and one sweep that says why the reading is a rank correlation rather than a slope.

The structural fact first, because it halves the problem. The rate gate settles 73 of the 114 scored rows before any feature is read: only 41 run at 176 400 Hz or above, 23 of them expecting 50k, 15 expecting `none` and 3 expecting 40k (`junkrules-r19-out.txt`, "rows_at_rate=41 expect_50k=23 expect_none=15 expect_other=3"). A ramp firing on those three costs nothing, because the lowest corner wins and 40k is lower than 50k; they read -0.9719, -0.9504 and -0.9442 on this rule in any case. So the rule has to separate 23 rows from 15, not 23 from 114.

The band bottom, 57.5 kHz, and the reading, the rank correlation of the band's 51-bin trend. Both come out of one sweep rather than out of a guess: 141 absolute band bottoms from 30.0 to 100.0 kHz at 0.5 kHz steps and 31 fractional ones from 0.30 to 0.90 of the top kept bin, each against five readings — signed fit quality, rank correlation, the same two over a 51-bin trend, and rank correlation over a 21-bin trend. 750 combinations in all, of which 9 separate the two groups at all, every one of them the trend rank, and the widest margin is 0.0090 at 57.5 kHz (`junkrules-r15-out.txt`, ITEM F). A least-squares fit cannot do it anywhere: its best margin is -0.0101, at 58.0 kHz, and the rank correlation of the unsmoothed band's best is -0.0028. That is the measurement the owner's ruling on question 1 ordered, run over the whole band it named.

The threshold, 0.963. The lowest row expecting 50k reads 0.9676 and the highest expecting `none` reads 0.9586, so 0.963 sits 0.0046 under the one and 0.0044 over the other (`junkrules-r19-out.txt`). That margin is thin and is named as thin: 0.009 of a correlation across 38 rows, the narrowest threshold in this plan by a wide distance. It is stated here rather than buried because it is the term most likely to move when the corpus grows, and because the alternative readings do not separate the groups at all rather than separating them by less.

Why monotonicity and not size or straightness. The two clean rows of `20260913T000949Z` rise as much as the weaker true ramps and fit a line about as well: on the band from 58 kHz they read fit quality 0.7316 and 0.6614 against the lowest true ramp's 0.7215, which is why every fit-quality threshold admits one group only by admitting the other. What they do not do is rise without interruption: their trend ranks 0.9356 and 0.9581 against 0.9676 for the weakest true ramp. Rise magnitude cannot do it either: the same `20260914T044230Z` row rises 8.8 dB on the 50 kHz band where the weakest true 50k row rises 3.15.

What the rule does not carry: a floor on the size of the rise. The rank correlation ignores size by construction, so a band that climbs 0.5 dB without interruption fires exactly as one that climbs 20 dB does. 2. The bin-count guard. `eligible` admits any spectrum of at least `SPUR_BASELINE_BINS` = 51 bins (`hqptuner/engine/junkadvisor.py:117-120`). The guard is raised to `DROP_TOP_BINS + SPUR_BASELINE_BINS` = 76 bins, so the curve the rules read still carries a full baseline.

What the raise does for the cliff, since the sentence above answers it only for the spur. At exactly 76 bins the kept curve is 51 bins wide and one bin spans `bandwidth / 75`, so whether the 20-26 kHz window holds a bin strictly inside it — both ends excluded, which is what the cliff rule needs — depends entirely on the bandwidth. Measured across the range `eligible` admits, `MIN_BANDWIDTH_HZ` = 24 000 Hz (`hqptuner/engine/junkadvisor.py:51`) upward, at 10 Hz steps (`junkrules-r21-out.txt`): the window holds no interior bin from 24.01 to 30.92 kHz, because the kept curve stops below 20 kHz there; it holds one to nine from 30.93 to 229.41 kHz; and above that it holds one only in patches — 230.77 to 260.00, 272.73 to 299.99, 333.34 to 354.54 and 428.58 to 433.33 kHz — and none at all above 433.34 kHz, where one bin step is wider than the window. So on a 76-bin grid the cliff rule fires over roughly 31 to 229 kHz of bandwidth and is silent outside it.

That is intended and is not qualified in the code. A 76-bin spectrum is the degenerate grid the guard exists to admit for the spur rule alone; the cliff going quiet there costs a verdict the grid cannot support, and adding a second guard for it would be a threshold with no reading behind it. Every row of the capture corpus carries 1025 bins, so no scored row is anywhere near this edge. A live stream of 51 to 75 bins stops producing capture rows the moment the guard goes to 76. That is the intended reading rather than a side effect — a row the detector cannot read is not a row worth capturing to tune the detector against — and it is named here because the plan lists `core/junkcal.py:59` among the sites that do not change while changing what it admits.
