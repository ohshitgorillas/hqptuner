### Pass 1, shape only

Re-run of the previous session's `shape` pass, with one statistic added and one phase in front of it. Phase A streams
every row and accumulates the level-above-floor histogram the contrast split is read off; it needs no contrast value.
The trough is read, and phase B streams the corpus again in the same process and scores every row with it. Per non-null
row, top 25 bins dropped:

- `floor` -- 10th percentile of the 9-bin median-smoothed curve, the row's own.
- `ref_rel` -- mean(15-18 kHz) minus floor.
- `ceiling_hz` -- highest bin strictly inside the shipped 20-26 kHz window standing CONTRAST_DB clear of the floor,
  else 0. The window is 20-26 kHz on the owner's ruling, not the 22-24 kHz the earlier draft carried (see "The window,
  corrected" below).
- `edge_rel` (NEW) -- the smoothed level at that ceiling bin minus the floor. How plainly the edge stands, with no
  reference band in it. This is the statistic a pair album's one kept cliff row is chosen by, because it is not the
  cliff math the ruling rejects.
- `depth`, `above_rel` -- mean(15-18 kHz) minus the MEDIAN of the band above the ceiling plus a 1.5 kHz guard, and
  that median above the floor. Recorded for every row; used as a class boundary for flat rows only.
- `spur`, `spur_hz` -- best per-bin excess of the raw curve over its 51-bin median baseline above 25 kHz, restricted
  to bins standing CONTRAST_DB clear of the floor, and the bin that carried it.
- `free_edge_hz` -- the highest bin anywhere in the row standing clear of the floor, with no window on it. Not a class
  boundary and no class reads it. It is the check on the window itself: an album whose rows carry no ceiling inside
  20-26 kHz but whose free edge sits below 20 kHz is band-limited lower than the window reaches, and the report names
  it with its number rather than calling it full-band by default.
- `slope`, `top_rel` -- 192 kHz rows only: least-squares dB/kHz from 25 kHz to the top of the surviving band, and
  mean(top 15%) minus floor.
- pass B, per file: the dominant spur bin (the bin the most of its spur-bearing rows agree on within 2 bins) and every
  row's level, baseline and excess read at that one bin.

### Marking a row

- ramp-positive: samplerate >= 176400 and slope > 0 and top_rel >= 3.
- **ramp wins.** A ramp-positive row is never cliff-positive, whatever its ceiling reads. 18 rows of
  20260912T235103Z (pairs, 192 kHz) are both under the raw statistics
  (`jq -r 'select(.sr>=176400 and .slope>0 and .top_rel>=3 and .ceiling_hz>0)' scores.jsonl`), and the two readings
  cannot both be true of the same content: a ramp needs energy rising to Nyquist, so content continues far above the
  ceiling the cliff element found, and that ceiling is rolloff inside a full-band signal rather than a band edge. The
  precedence is one-way and costs nothing in the other direction: band-limited 192 kHz material (fake hires) has no
  top-band energy, cannot be ramp-positive, and is still marked cliff. With the precedence applied the two
  segmentations can no longer claim the same row, so no piece is claimed by both, no piece takes two classes, and the
  `label_only` branch can no longer swallow a piece that was cut for its ramp.
- cliff-positive, flat row: depth >= 30 and not ramp-positive.
- cliff-positive, pair row: `ceiling_hz > 0` and not ramp-positive -- a content edge inside 20-26 kHz standing clear of the row's own floor,
  with the band above it read below that edge. Presence only; no depth, no reference band. Every album marked this way
  is `label_only`.
- spur-positive: spur >= 22. Never cuts a file.

### Cutting a file into albums

Per file, per signature (cliff, ramp), in row order over non-null rows:

1. maximal runs of positives, absorbing a stretch shorter than `GAP` inside a run ONLY where every row in it is
   positive for NEITHER signature (the owner's ruling on the first emit). A stretch holding a row of the other
   signature is not bridged, and the run ends there. Bridging across the other signature is what lost the head of
   `junkcal-20260912T235103Z.jsonl` (143 rows, 1 null), whose first 20 rows carry 19 cliff positives: they were
   absorbed into a cliff run spanning the whole file, that run then lost the overlap contest to the file's 63 ramp
   positives, and the head fell out unmarked and was written as a `clean` album 95% cliff-positive. The owner named it
   by the piece stamp of its first row, "the 235118 head run"; the capture is 20260912T235103Z and no file in the
   corpus is named 235118. With the ruling applied that head run stands on its own and cuts;
2. a surviving run carrying fewer than `MIN_RUN` POSITIVE rows is not a run and leaves its rows where they lie.
   `MIN_RUN` counts positives, never the span the absorption in step 1 produced: a cluster of three positives spread
   over six rows carries three, not six;
3. the file is cut at every surviving run boundary; an unmarked remainder shorter than `MIN_RUN` is absorbed into the
   neighbouring piece it touches, the earlier one when it touches two.

Each piece is an album named by its first row's timestamp as `YYYYmmddTHHMMSSZ`, the spelling `_claim` gives a capture
file. `GAP` and `MIN_RUN` are ASSUMED, tagged below: they define what an album is, and every piece, class, role and

### Class, per piece

Cliff piece -> `cliff`; ramp piece -> `ramp`. An unmarked piece is `spur` when its dominant spur bin is positive in at
least half its rows, otherwise `clean`. A cliff or ramp piece that also carries a persistent spur keeps its class and
also keeps the spur row.

Implied filter: cliff -> `20k`, ramp -> `50k`, clean -> `none`, spur -> `40k` when the dominant bin is strictly above
45 000.0 Hz and `30k` at or below it, the shipped rule at the corner line.

**Lossy sub-case, the owner's ruling on the first emit.** Content that stops below 20 kHz is lossy sub-case material; a
`20k` cut does nothing there, so the class is excised: such an album is `clean` with filter `none`, never `cliff`/`20k`,
and its rows carry `free_edge_hz` so a junkrules test can bite on a wrong implementation that still says `20k`. The
test is agreement across the piece, and agreement is what separates band-limited content from a near-silent row whose
free edge wanders. A row carrying NO free edge at all (`free_edge_hz == 0.0`, silence throughout) counts toward neither
side of the fraction: it is evidence of nothing either way, and reading it as "below 20 kHz" would make any silent
stretch lossy by arithmetic.
