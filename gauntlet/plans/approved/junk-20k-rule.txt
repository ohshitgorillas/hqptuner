## What is wrong

The engine's 20k rule is not the locked rule. `hqptuner/engine/junkadvisor.py:301-317` reads a content edge inside 20 to 26 kHz off the 30 s window minimum and fires on the reference band minus the median above that edge, `:309` `ref = _band_mean(curve.smoothed, curve.at(CLIFF_REF_HZ[0]), curve.at(CLIFF_REF_HZ[1]))`. The resource note names that statistic as the thing to replace: `docs/junk-filter-autopilot-resource-20k.md:129` "the engine classifies on the 30 s block minimum through the reference-band fall", and `:269` lists this slug's content, "mask, loud-frame step, veto with yield, per-block verdict in `junkadvisor.py`, replacing the reference-band fall".

Two defects behind it, both measured on the corpus. The edge finder walks past a hard wall: `:173` "some bin at the 26 kHz window top clears the floor plus `CONTRAST_DB` ... so `_content_edge` walks past the wall to the window top and returns None", 69 blocks with no reading at all. And the fall is capped by passage level: `:173` "a quiet passage where the reference sits near the dither floor, so a reference-band fall is capped by passage level; that cap is the statistic's limit, and the reason the floor step replaced it".

The locked rule reads a closed block, not a window. `docs/junk-filter-autopilot-resource-20k.md:111`: "block kept at or above -125 dB per Hz above the fold, loud-frame step over its cut, and either the ratio to the music under -15 dB or the step at or over the 14 dB yield". Its four inputs exist in the engine already and nothing reads them: `hqptuner/engine/metering.py:162` `latest_block()`, "with no consumer yet" (`docs/junk-filter-autopilot-resource-20k.md:251`).

One record field is not what its module says it is. `hqptuner/engine/blockstats.py:9` claims the functions mirror "``jbedge.p90_curve``"; `:119` `p90=[float(v) for v in np.percentile(frames[:, : grid.kept], 90, axis=0)]` is the raw percentile, while `scripts/junkburst/jbedge.py:90-93` runs that percentile through `median_smooth(..., SMOOTH_BINS)` before returning. Measured spread: `docs/junk-filter-autopilot-resource-20k.md:260` "the two differ by 8 to 26 dB per bin, widest at 192 kHz", and "the module docstring claims the record mirrors `p90_curve` and it does not".

One closed block can be empty. `hqptuner/engine/metering.py:156` appends a record only `if self._rows`, so a block of frames all under the RMS gate closes carrying nothing and `latest_block()` goes on returning the block before it. Nothing reads it today, so the staleness is inert; a rule reading it is a verdict with memory, which `gauntlet/plans/approved/junk-live-verdict.txt:12` forbids: "No time-averaging and no memory of past verdicts survives anywhere in the detection path."

## What the owner sees change

The chip's 20k note starts and stops on different evidence. Today it needs content that stops inside 20 to 26 kHz on the 30 s minimum with a 30 dB fall below the 15 to 18 kHz band; after this it needs one closed second whose band above the fold stands at or above -125 dB per Hz, whose loud-frame step clears 11 dB, and whose ratio to the music clears the veto or whose step reaches 14 dB.

Three audible consequences on the corpus's own albums. Soft walls now fire: Foundations of Burden and Wine Dark Sea, `docs/junk-filter-autopilot-resource-20k.md:125`, "a 20 to 25 dB fall over two to three kHz at 22 and 24 kHz". Junk that sits far below the music stops firing, because the mask drops the block: `:56` "a higher line never adds a costly error and only lowers the junk it is willing to leave". And real hi-res whose ultrasonics track the music is protected by the veto: `:109` "Under the policy no rule with the -15 dB veto engages a real burst anywhere in the corpus".

The note also arrives up to one block later than today and changes at block rate. Both are under What it costs.

The 20k reason line is rewritten, owner-approved this round, and the number in it now means the fold rather than a measured wall: change 1c.

## What it costs

**Tolerance against the scoring package.** 1e-4 dB per bin and per scalar, `docs/junk-filter-autopilot-resource-20k.md:249`: "the port tolerance is 1e-4 dB per bin and per scalar, an order over the largest reading", the same tolerance the shipped record already meets at `:260`. It is stated here over the smoothed p90 curve and the loud-frame step, and over the block's boolean call it must agree exactly, a boolean having no tolerance. Measured at stage 2 by the `examiner` over one stored burst each at 88.2, 96 and 192 kHz from `/srv/hqptuner/state/junkburst/derived/`, read-only, every non-silent frame of five closed blocks per burst against `jbedge.g90_value` and `jbpolicyyield._fixed_yield_calls` on the same frames. Those three rates are the whole corpus: `:276` "The corpus holds no 44.1 or 48 kHz bursts: 72 at 88.2 kHz, 338 at 96 kHz, 118 at 192 kHz."

**A verdict that changes block by block.** Blocks close every second (`hqptuner/engine/metering.py:59` `BLOCK_SECONDS = 1.0`) and the rule reads only the latest one, so the 20k verdict is a per-second verdict with no memory, and after change 3b a silent second clears it outright. Auto-pilot follows it directly and is forbidden hysteresis and any hold timer (`gauntlet/plans/approved/junk-live-verdict.txt:14`), and it samples at the status poll, 2.0 s by default (`hqptuner/config.py:81`, `hqptuner/api/lifespan.py:78`), so it sees every other block. It writes only on a change (`hqptuner/core/autopilotops.py:47-53`, "the ordinary case of the engaged filter already being the wanted one"), and each change is a readback-verified, event-logged `SetJunkFilter` through the LIVE lane (`hqptuner/core/autopilotops.py:1-13`). A track sitting near the cut therefore engages and releases the junk filter as often as once every 2 s, and a dropped block reads `OFF` whatever the step says (`docs/junk-filter-autopilot-resource-20k.md:56`), so a mask that drops alternate blocks releases an engaged filter mid-wall. The corpus shows exactly that shape: `:105` names three bursts whose "kept blocks 1 and 4, 2 and 4, 0 and 2" never fall two in a row. The confirming block and the hold that damp this are the run rule, the next slug (`:270`), and the brief leaves both out of this one. Until it lands, the engine runs the rule bare.

**A block read on few frames.** The RMS gate keeps silent frames out of the record (`hqptuner/engine/metering.py:146`, `:61-64`), so a block that carries one surviving frame has a `p90` equal to that frame, where the 11 dB cut and the 14 dB yield were read over roughly 94 frames per second (`docs/junk-filter-autopilot-resource-20k.md:123`). The rule adds no frame-count guard and reads such a block like any other. That geometry is unmeasured by the corpus rather than tested against it: `:276` "No burst in the port sample carried a silent frame under the engine's RMS gate, so the silent path of the port is unmeasured." The guard against a single wrong block is the run rule's confirming block, which this slug does not carry.

**Coverage, and a later note.** The 20k note waits for the first closed block, up to 1 s after the stream starts, where today the first folded frame already carries a readable spectrum (`hqptuner/engine/junkadvisor.py:161-164`). The spur and ramp rules are unaffected; they keep reading `window_min_db()`.

**Grading moves off the fixture.** The junkcal corpus can no longer grade the 20k rule: 17 of its rows lose their scorer (change 4), and the two replay scripts that carry no frames lose their 20k column with it (change 5). What still grades the shipped rule is the burst corpus under `/srv/hqptuner/state/junkburst/`, through `jbscore.py` and `jblabelled.py` once both pass a record. Nothing inside the test suite pins the mirror; that oracle fixture is its own slug and is open (`docs/junk-filter-autopilot-resource-20k.md:262`, `:272`).
