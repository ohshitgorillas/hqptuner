What is wrong
The detector reads the per-bin minimum over six full 5 s blocks (metering.py:58-59, :166), so no verdict exists before 30 s of covered playback, and a bin's value is the quietest 93 ms hop anywhere in the last 30 s. The approved plan junk-instant-spectrum (plans/approved/junk-instant-spectrum.txt:1-12) answered that by reading one hop with no minimum at all; it was never implemented (HEAD metering.py:119-172 still carries the blocks, and `frame_db` does not exist). The owner's brief replaces both: a minimum, but over one second of coverage.

What the user sees change
- The advisor chip and auto-pilot react after about one second of non-silent playback instead of about thirty.
- A verdict now follows the last second of the music rather than the last thirty, so the chip and auto-pilot's junk filter move on second scales where the signature comes and goes.
- The junkcal capture's `spectrum` rows carry the 1 s minimum from the first row after ~1 s instead of null for the first ~30 s (junkcal.txt:23, :45).

Design, one window
`SpectralAggregate` keeps `bins`, `bandwidth`, `frames`, `seconds` as now (metering.py:131-134; `seconds` and `frames` still count every ingested hop, silent ones included, since junkcal stores them at junkcal.py:69-70). The block fields are replaced by one deque `_hops` of `(mags_sq, covered_seconds)` plus a running `_coverage` sum of its contents. `add()` keeps the frame and seconds counters; for a non-silent hop it appends and then pops from the left while `_coverage - oldest.covered_seconds >= WINDOW_SECONDS`, so the deque is the shortest suffix of non-silent hops whose coverage reaches one second. A silent hop does nothing to the deque: it is not pushed and its coverage is not counted, which is what holds it out of the minimum without letting it age the window. `window_min_db()` returns None while `_coverage < WINDOW_SECONDS`, else the per-bin minimum over the deque in dB, exactly the conversion at metering.py:172. Coverage is measured on the wire's `transformTime` (protocol.md:291, `= hop/rate`), so at a 2048-sample transform (protocol.md:291, observed 1025 bins) the window holds 11 ingested hops at 44.1 kHz (1.022 s), 24 at 96 kHz (1.024 s), 44 at 176.4 kHz (1.022 s) and 47 at 192 kHz (1.003 s), and any other hop length the daemon sends is handled by the same sum with no rate table. Memory is at most 47 × bins floats per aggregate; the minimum is recomputed on each `window_min_db()` call, as today.

Through digital silence mid-track the deque is untouched, so the verdict is the last second of non-silent audio, the same standing a block window gave it. A track change or a broken stream still discards the aggregate (metering.py:276, :339-341); a pause keeps it (metering.py:271).

Cost per rule against a 1 s minimum
Common to all three: a per-bin minimum over N noise hops sinks as N grows. Modelling a stationary noise bin's per-hop power as exponential, the expected minimum sits 10·log10(N) below the per-hop mean, so at 96 kHz the noise bands of a 1 s minimum (24 hops) sit about 15 dB higher than in the 30 s minimum (about 700 hops). ASSUMED: the model, not the count; settling it needs a live capture of per-hop spectra, and the existing junkcal rows store only the 30 s minimum (junkcal.py:43-49). Music bands rise too, since fewer quiet moments fall inside a second. Every rule compares bands or bins of the same curve to its own floor (junkadvisor.py:108-109, :248), so the common part cancels; what does not cancel is stated per rule. Thresholds stay as the brief orders.
- 20k (`_ceiling_wall`, junkadvisor.py:218-232; needs a rate of 88 200 or more, a content edge strictly inside 20 to 26 kHz above floor+8 dB, the band from edge+1.5 kHz up at or under floor+8 dB, and the 15 to 18 kHz mean at least 30 dB above that band). False firing: a real hi-res track whose content above its 20 to 26 kHz edge falls silent for one full second while its 15 to 18 kHz band stays 30 dB above the ultrasonic floor now reads as brick-walled for that second; at 30 s it needed thirty seconds of that. The two conditions pull against each other in real music (ultrasonic energy rides on the same transients that fill 15 to 18 kHz), so the exposure is sustained-treble passages with no ultrasonic content, and it lasts as long as the passage plus one second. Drop-out: the cliff of a true fake hi-res source is in every hop, so what drops the verdict is the reference band: at 30 s one quiet 93 ms hop anywhere in the window pulled 15 to 18 kHz down for the next thirty seconds; at 1 s a quiet moment costs one second. Drop-outs are shorter and more frequent, tracking the music's own treble envelope. The uncancelled part is the ~15 dB higher ultrasonic noise band, which narrows the measured cliff: a fake hi-res track whose 15 to 18 kHz minimum clears the floor by between 30 and roughly 45 dB fired at 30 s and does not at 1 s.
- spur (`_spurs`, junkadvisor.py:235-266; a raw bin above 25 kHz at least 15 dB over the 51-bin median of the same curve and 8 dB over its floor; only containers wider than 25 kHz, so rates of 88 200 and up). False firing: this is the rule the 30 s window was written for (metering.py:52-57): a narrow musical partial above 25 kHz held for one second now reads as a persistent tone and recommends 30k or 40k for as long as it holds plus one second; at 30 s no musical partial lasted long enough. Noise cannot fake it at either length: a noise bin's minimum over 24 hops lies at or under any single sample, and 15 dB over the median of neighbouring minima is not reached. Drop-out: a bias tone is in every hop and survives any minimum; the verdict drops when the local baseline of the last second rises to within 15 dB of it, which is sustained loud broadband content around the tone's frequency for a full second. The 30 s minimum's baseline was the quietest second of thirty, so this is more frequent than before and less frequent than the mean-spectrum case the module records (junkadvisor.py:30-31, a 15 dB tone falling to 6 dB of excess); the tone's measured excess sits between those two.
- 50k (`_ramp`, junkadvisor.py:269-283; bandwidth over 50 kHz, so 176.4 kHz and up; the top 15 % of the band at least 10 dB above the band just over 25 kHz and 20 dB above the floor). False firing: the rule needs energy rising with frequency up to the top of a 88 kHz or wider band for a full second; music decays with frequency, so no ordinary passage produces it at 1 s any more than at 30 s. Drop-out: a shaping ramp is continuous noise, in every hop; both bands the rule compares are noise, so the 10·log10(N) shift cancels in the rise, and the floor+20 dB test moves with the floor. Per-bin scatter is larger over 44 hops than over ~1 300 but the 9-bin median (junkadvisor.py:108, `SMOOTH_BINS` at :53) and the band means absorb it. No change worth stating in either direction.
- Write path: with no debounce, every verdict flip auto-pilot sees at its tick becomes one LIVE-lane `apply_now` (autopilotops.py:81), bounded by one write per `poll_interval` (config.py:81, default 2.0 s), so at most about 1 800 an hour under a signature flickering every tick and none while it holds. The write costs the stream nothing (architecture.md:99, manual §2.8), so flicker is a log and audit cost, not an audible one.
