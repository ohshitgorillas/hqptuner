slug: junk-live-verdict
grounding: detective

brief:
Rewrite the junk-filter detector's 20k and 50k rules to read the windowed minimum spectrum (`window_min_db()`), as the spur rule already does. Both rules currently read a mean spectrum accumulated over time; that accumulator goes, and so does the verdict latch: a verdict is a property of the current spectrum, recomputed every tick, held by nothing. The "fake lossy hi-res" case goes with it: a source that brick-walls below 20 kHz gets no verdict, because the 20k filter does nothing to it.

Scope
- In: brick-wall and ramp rules, the mean accumulator in `SpectralAggregate`, the latch in `MeteringReader.verdict`, and whatever in the advisor note or auto-pilot follows from a live verdict.
- Out: the spur rule (30k/40k), rate-relative filters, the junkcal capture format, threshold values already set.

Rulings
- No time-averaging and no memory of past verdicts survives anywhere in the detection path. Two structures are permitted and no other: the rolling per-bin minimum the brief names as the rules' input, over whatever reach the current plans set, and statistics over one closed block of 1 s of coverage, that block's per-bin minimum, its per-bin 90th percentile and the band scalars read off its own frames. Nothing else spanning more than one block, and no memory of a past verdict, is.
- A filter is recommended, and auto-pilot engages it, only when it has an effect on what is playing. 20k needs a content ceiling above 20 kHz with near-floor energy above it. 50k needs a container whose bandwidth reaches past the corner; derive the rate floor from that and state it.
- Auto-pilot follows the live verdict directly: engage when present, release when absent. No hysteresis, no hold timer.

Evidence
- `/srv/hqptuner/state/junkcal/*.jsonl` holds 21 periods of the windowed minimum spectrum from 13 kHz up; `scripts/junkcal_view.py` reads it. Use it to show the rewritten rules fire where the curves show the shape and stay quiet where they do not. Recorded verdicts in the rows are the old rules' output, not ground truth.

- The unreleased changelog entry about the content ceiling is superseded; say what happens to it.

what is wrong

The detector has three rules and two curves. The spur rule reads the windowed per-bin minimum spectrum (`junkadvisor.py:258-289`, fed from `metering.py:167`). The brick-wall and ramp rules read a power mean accumulated over every frame ingested since the current item started (`metering.py:163-165`, consumed at `metering.py:241`). Three consequences:

1. The mean's answer depends on the passage. The module docstring already records the failure it caused for the spur rule — a 30.3 kHz tone 15 dB proud during a quiet intro fell to 6 dB of excess once the music started (`junkadvisor.py:33-38`) — and the brick-wall rule is exposed to the same thing from the other side: a loud passage lifts the near-floor band above the ceiling and the cliff shallows out.

2. The verdict latches for the rest of the item (`metering.py:249-250`). A verdict therefore outlives the evidence for it, and auto-pilot holds a filter the current spectrum no longer asks for.

3. `_lossy_wall` (`junkadvisor.py:237-248`) accepts a ceiling anywhere in 13–18 kHz (`LOSSY_WINDOW_HZ`, `junkadvisor.py:72`) and recommends the 20k corner for it. The 20k corner removes nothing below 20 kHz, so that recommendation cannot change what the listener hears.

what changes

1. `hqptuner/engine/junkadvisor.py`

1a. `classify` (`:94-122`) loses `levels_db` and `seconds` and takes the windowed minimum as its single spectrum. It returns None when that spectrum is None, which is the only readiness gate left. All three rules then read one curve: the 9-bin median smoothing for brick-wall and ramp, the raw per-bin values for spurs, exactly as the spur rule already does (`:264-268`). The `# noqa: PLR0913` at `:94` goes with the argument count.

1b. `eligible` (`:124-128`) loses `seconds`, and `MIN_SECONDS` (`:49`) is deleted. Its 15 s coverage floor is dead once the windowed minimum is the input: that window does not exist until six full 5 s blocks have been earned (`metering.py:167-176`, `BLOCK_SECONDS`/`WINDOW_BLOCKS` at `metering.py:57-59`), which is 30 s, twice the floor. The rate, bandwidth, bin-count and SDM tests in `eligible` stay as they are.

1c. The brick-wall rule keeps one path instead of two. `BRICK_WINDOW_HZ` (`:63`) moves from an 18–26 kHz search window to 20–26 kHz. `_content_edge` (`:193-205`) changes with it, and the change is a behaviour change: today it returns the highest bin standing clear of the floor anywhere in the window, its bottom bin included, rejecting only a bin at or above the window top (`:198-205`). With the bottom at 20 kHz that would report a ceiling at exactly 20.0 kHz, where the 20k corner removes nothing. It gains a strict-bottom test — the ceiling bin must sit above the window's bottom bin — which is the "content ceiling above 20 kHz" half of the ruling. The two edges then read: a ceiling at exactly 20.0 kHz is refused by the new test, and one at 26.0 kHz is refused by the existing `edge >= top` test at `:205`, which is content carrying on past the window rather than a ceiling. `_at_floor_above` (`:208-210`) is unchanged and supplies the "near-floor energy above it" half as it already does. `_lossy_wall` (`:237-248`) and `LOSSY_WINDOW_HZ` (`:72`) are deleted. `_ceiling_wall` (`:222-235`) is the surviving rule body; the dispatcher `_brick_wall` (`:251-255`) has nothing left to dispatch between and collapses into it, carrying the rate test at `:252` with it. `FAKE_HIRES_MIN_RATE` (`:68`) keeps its value of 88 200 Hz and keeps being tested: it is a threshold already set and the brief's Out list excludes those, and it is what keeps the unchanged 20k reason's "consistent with fake hi-res" wording (`:213-219`) true of every source that reaches the rule. So the narrowest container that reaches it is still 88.2 kHz, Nyquist 44.1 kHz, where the band `_at_floor_above` averages runs from above 21.5 kHz to 44.1 kHz — on the 1 025-bin grid the captures show, roughly half the curve, not a handful of bins. `BRICK_DROP_DB`, `BRICK_GUARD_HZ`, `ABOVE_FLOOR_DB` and `BRICK_REF_HZ` keep their values.

1d. The ramp rule gains one precondition: the source bandwidth must exceed 50 kHz. Derivation: the bandwidth the frame header carries is the source Nyquist (`protocol.md:291`, "`f32 bandwidth` (Nyquist, Hz)"; header layout at `metering.py:38`, read out at `metering.py:353`), so bandwidth above the 50 kHz corner means a sample rate above 100 kHz. The lowest standard PCM rate that clears 100 kHz is 176.4 kHz, so the 50k filter is never recommended for an 88.2 or 96 kHz source, and the effective rate floor for this rule is 176 400 Hz. The rule is stated on bandwidth rather than on the rate because bandwidth is the fact the curve is measured against; the rate floor follows from it and is stated here because the brief asks for it. The precondition reads strictly at its edge: a container whose bandwidth is exactly 50 kHz is refused, because a 50 kHz corner removes nothing from it. That edge is unreachable in practice — no standard PCM rate is 100 kHz — so the first rate that passes is 176.4 kHz and the first bandwidth that passes is 88.2 kHz, well clear of the edge. `RAMP_LO_HZ`, `RAMP_RISE_DB` and `RAMP_ABOVE_FLOOR_DB` keep their values.

1e. The module docstring (`:1-45`) is rewritten: it currently explains at `:26-38` why the brick-wall and ramp rules read the mean and only the spur rule reads the minimum, which is the design being removed.

1f. No user-facing string changes. The 20k reason (`_fake_hires`, `:213-219`) and the 50k reason (inside `_ramp`, `:300-304`) ship character for character as they are, and the spur reason is out of scope. Nothing in this change reworded them.

The removed 20k case. No recorded 20k verdict in any of the 20 periods has a ceiling below 20 kHz — the recorded ceilings run 22.0 to 25.9 kHz — so the captures contain no instance of the case change 1c removes, and its removal changes no tick in this evidence. Flapping, measured. Removing the latch makes the verdict change between ticks. Over the 2 074 eligible ticks the rewritten detector changes its answer 258 times against the recorded rows' 17. The captures tick every 15 s (`junkcal.py:36`) while auto-pilot decides every `poll_interval`, 2.0 s by default (`config.py:81`, handed over at `api/lifespan.py:78`), so 258 is a lower bound on how often auto-pilot would move the filter over those 8.6 hours of playback, not an estimate of it. Most of that churn is not from the rewritten rules: the five periods where the rewritten 20k and 50k rules are almost or entirely silent (`062936Z`, `083210Z`, `161658Z`, `021159Z`, `032631Z`) account for 183 of the 258 changes, and in those five the 20k rule fires 8 times and the 50k rule 3. The rest is the spur rule's own verdict appearing and clearing on a rolling window — unchanged code, previously hidden by the latch and now visible to auto-pilot. The brief rules out hysteresis and a hold timer, so this is a disclosed cost of the change rather than a problem to be solved here.

Measurement caveat, bounded. The captures store the minimum spectrum only from 13 kHz up (`junkcal.py:42-47`), while the rules take their floor percentile over the whole curve from 0 Hz. My evaluator therefore took the floor over the stored slice. I also ran the alternative convention — the order statistic a full-length curve would select if every bin below 13 kHz ranked above the decile, which is the direction music energy pushes it — and the two conventions disagree on the final verdict for 11 of the 2 074 ticks (6 in `232043Z`, 3 in `021159Z`, 2 in `032631Z`). Every count above is from the first convention and is right to within those 11 ticks.
