## What is wrong

Block closure throws the block away and keeps one array. `metering.py:154-158` pushes `_block_min` and drops everything else the block saw; the frames themselves are never held (`:146-152` folds each into the running min). No per-bin percentile and no band scalar can be read at runtime, because the frames they read are gone one hop after arrival.

The corpus the auto-pilot grades on reads exactly those numbers per block: `docs/junk-filter-autopilot-resource.md:33` — "the block median across frames of the mean level over the band from 300 Hz to 6 kHz above the fold, at whichever of 22.05 and 24 kHz reads higher; the music level the same over 15 to 18 kHz; the ratio their difference", over "1612 steady 1 s blocks".

Two geometry mismatches against that corpus. Block length: `metering.py:59` `BLOCK_SECONDS = 5.0`, corpus headline block `scripts/junkburst/jbconfig.py:82` `HEADLINE_WINDOW = 1.0`. Frame rate: `metering.py:48` `DECIMATE = 4` ingests one hop in four, while the stored bursts hold every frame — `scripts/junkcal_burst.py:12` "nothing decimated, nothing thresholded, no minimum folded" — so every cut locked in that document was read at full rate over five times the block resolution the engine keeps.

Third mismatch, the one the second brief turns on: the arithmetic itself. The engine carries no numpy (`pyproject.toml:6-13`; no `import numpy` anywhere under `hqptuner/`, 23 import sites across 22 files under `scripts/`, all of them tooling), so a scalar twin of the scoring package was the only shape available, and a scalar twin is two implementations of one rule.
