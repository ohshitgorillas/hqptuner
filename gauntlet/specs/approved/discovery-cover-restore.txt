Wire and protocol facts:

- The request is one UDP datagram, byte for byte `<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?><discover>hqplayer</discover>`, sent to multicast 239.192.0.199 port 4321 (docs/protocol.md:26-29). A `:port` suffix on the configured target overrides the 4321.
- The reply is `<discover result="OK" name="friendly name" version="...">hqplayer</discover>` (docs/protocol.md:35). The daemon's address is the datagram's own sender address, never a field of the body: the body is not documented to carry one.
- The reply's `version` is the major only, `Signalyst HQPlayer Embedded 6`; no Control API command carries the installed release number (docs/protocol.md:165).
- Discovery is optional and answers on IPv4 and IPv6 (`ff08::c7`); only the IPv4 group is sent on, so a daemon answering only on IPv6 is absent from the list rather than an error (docs/protocol.md:26).
- Port 4321 refuses connections for about ten seconds after a daemon restart and answers `GetInfo` 9.3 s in (docs/protocol.md:21), so a daemon can answer the datagram and refuse the control socket in the same breath; the record stays, with product and platform unfilled.
- `GetInfo` answers the daemon's identity attributes on the same 4321 connection (docs/protocol.md §1).

Applicable fixtures:

- `spawn_threaded_daemon` (tests/conftest.py:316) — the control-daemon fake served from a background thread over real loopback TCP on an ephemeral port, reachable from the loop `TestClient` runs the app on; `overrides`, `state` and `log` let a sync test steer and watch the daemon's side of the wire.
- `fake_control.serve` / `_query` (tests/support/fake_control.py:335) — answers `GetInfo` as `<GetInfo name="Fake" engine="6.0.4" version="6"/>`, carrying no product and no platform attribute. Line 1 asserts four of its five values against this reply, so the fake's `GetInfo` gains `product` and `platform`, by the writer's hand, in the test tree. `GetInfo`'s attribute set is `name`, `product`, `version`, `platform`, `engine` (docs/protocol.md:157), and the verified 6.0.4 response is `<GetInfo engine="6.0.4" name="Opal" platform="Linux" product="Signalyst HQPlayer Embedded" version="6"/>` (docs/protocol.md:160), which is the shape the fake's reply follows.
- `_closed_port` (tests/conftest.py:394) — binds a loopback port, reads the number, hands back the hole, so a test can name an address that refuses at once.
- `virtual_clock` (tests/conftest.py:100, autouse) — virtualizes `ConnectionManager.sleep` and `ConnectionManager.monotonic` and nothing else, so it does not reach the search; a test that needs virtual time here injects its own pair through the entry point.
- No datagram fake exists anywhere under `tests/`: `SOCK_DGRAM`, `create_datagram_endpoint` and `sock_recvfrom` appear only in the production module.
